## PREMISE

It is Eid morning. Uncle Rashid — the elder from the house across from yours — calls out from the street. He is already dressed in his white kandura. He is holding a tray of sweets his wife made yesterday.

Five interactions. One festival day. The difference between a polite visit and being declared family.

---

## CHARACTER: Uncle Rashid

Emirati man, mid-sixties. Long-time resident. Generous, patient, keenly attuned to social signals. Has welcomed dozens of expats over the years — knows who made the effort.

**His wife Umm Mohammed** appears briefly in Scene 3 (coffee/sweets service) — this is the chaperone presence required for female learners to be alone in the home with the elder.

**Address by learner gender:**
- Male learner: ولدي (waladī, "my son")
- Female learner: بنتي (bintī, "my daughter")

## SCENE 01 — The Eid Greeting

**Setting:** Your doorstep. Rashid stands in the street, smiling.

**Rashid:** عيدكم مبارك! تعالوا عندنا! *(ʿīdkum mubārak! taʿālū ʿindnā!)*

**Teaching:** عيدكم مبارك is the Eid-specific greeting. كل عام وأنتم بخير is the year-end / general one — using it on Eid morning marks you as someone who learned phrases but not occasions. The traditional follow-up وعساكم من عوّاده is what most non-natives never learn.

| Choice | Arabic | Romanization | Pts | Warmth |
|--------|--------|--------------|-----|--------|
| A | عيدكم مبارك وعساكم مِن عوّاده! تَشَرَّفنا والله | ʿīdkum mubārak w-ʿasākum min ʿawwāda! tasharrafnā wallah | +3 | +15 |
| B | عيدكم مبارك! إن شاء الله | ʿīdkum mubārak! in shāʾ Allāh | +2 | +8 |
| C | كل عام وانتم بخير | kull ʿām w-intum bi-khayr | 0 | -2 |
| D | مشكور! يمكن بعدين — عندي خطّة | mashkūr! yimkin baʿdēn — ʿindī khuṭṭa | -2 | -15 |

→ A/B/C → **Scene 2** | D → **Scene 5-Cold (skip middle scenes)**

## SCENE 02 — Entering the Home / The Coffee First ⚠️ DIVERGENCE SCENE

**Setting:** Rashid's living room. His wife enters carrying a small dallah (Arabic coffee pot) and finjan cups. She greets you, places coffee on the table, sits briefly with you.

**Rashid:** تَفَضَّل/تَفَضَّلي — قهوة أوّل شي *(tafaḍḍal/tafaḍḍali — gahwa awwal shay)*
**Umm Mohammed:** أهلاً وسهلاً، عيدكم مبارك

**Teaching:** In Gulf hospitality, Arabic coffee comes first — always. The correct sequence is: coffee, then dates/sweets, then anything else. Acknowledging both hosts (not just Rashid) is the cultural mastery move.

| Choice | Arabic | Romanization | Pts | Warmth |
|--------|--------|--------------|-----|--------|
| A | بسم الله — الله يَعطيكم العافية، عيدكم مبارك يا أم محمد | bismi-llāh — Allāh yiʿṭīkum il-ʿāfiya, ʿīdkum mubārak yā Umm Moḥammed | +3 | +15 |
| B | بسم الله — مشكور يا عمّي | bismi-llāh — mashkūr yā ʿammī | +2 | +8 |
| C ⚠️ | Thank you, the coffee smells nice | shukran, riḥtahā ḥilwa | **0** | **-5** |
| D | لا شكراً، ما أحبّ القهوة | lā shukran, mā aḥibb il-gahwa | -2 | -15 |

**⚠️ Choice C divergence:** acceptable Arabic compliment but addressed to no one specifically — wife is ignored, hospitality not acknowledged in cultural register.

→ **Scene 3**

## SCENE 03 — The Sweets

**Setting:** Wife returns with tray of homemade Eid sweets (maamoul, balaleet, luqaimat).

**Rashid:** زوجتي سَوّاتها مِن أمس. ذوق وقُل/قولي رأيَك *(zōjtī sawwāthā min ams. dhūq wa-gul/gūlī raʾyak)*

**Teaching:** Asking who made them honours the maker's labour. "يسلموا إيديها" (bless her hands) said in front of her is the cultural peak.

| Choice | Arabic | Romanization | Pts | Warmth |
|--------|--------|--------------|-----|--------|
| A | ما شاء الله! مين سوّاها؟ يسلَموا إيديها — الله يَعطيكِ العافية | mā shāʾ Allāh! mīn sawwāhā? yislamū īdīhā — Allāh yiʿṭīch il-ʿāfiya | +3 | +15 |
| B | يسلَموا إيديها يا عمّي | yislamū īdīhā yā ʿammī | +2 | +10 |
| C | شكراً! شَكلها لذيذة | shukran! shaklhā ladhīdha | 0 | +2 |
| D | لا شكراً، أنا محافظ/ـة على السكّر | lā shukran, ana muḥāfiẓ/ẓa ʿalā s-sukkar | -2 | -15 |

→ **Scene 4**

## SCENE 04 — Asking About Family

**Setting:** Wife leaves to greet other neighbours. Rashid leans back, comfortable.

**Rashid:** وانت/انتي — العايلة هني ولا في البلاد؟ *(w-inta/inti — il-ʿāyla hinī walla fī l-blād?)*

**Teaching:** Family questions on Eid are not small talk — Rashid is asking if you have somewhere to go today. The right answer reciprocates: you share, then ask about his.

| Choice | Arabic | Romanization | Pts | Warmth |
|--------|--------|--------------|-----|--------|
| A | في البلاد، الحمد لله. وانت يا عمّي؟ كم ولد عندَك؟ | fī l-blād, al-ḥamdu li-llāh. w-inta yā ʿammī? kam walad ʿindak? | +3 | +15 |
| B | في البلاد. الله يَحفَظهم | fī l-blād. Allāh yiḥfaẓhum | +2 | +10 |
| C | عندي عيلة بس بعيدين | ʿindī ʿāyla bass biʿīdīn | 0 | 0 |
| D | أُفَضِّل ما أتكلّم عن العايلة | ufaḍḍil mā atkallam ʿan il-ʿāyla | -2 | -12 |

## SCENE 05 — The Farewell

**Setting:** Doorstep. Rashid walks you out personally.

**Rashid (warm):** الله يبارك فيك يا ولدي/بنتي. والله انبَسَطنا فيك اليوم. بيتنا بيتك دايماً — ما هو كلام

**Teaching:** "بيتنا بيتك دايماً" is a formal declaration of belonging. It needs an answer of equal weight. Generic thanks here is like pocketing a gift without looking at it.

| Choice | Arabic | Romanization | Pts | Warmth |

## ENDINGS

### Exceptional — Adopted Family
**أنت ولدنا / أنتِ بنتنا** — Points ≥ 13, Warmth ≥ 75 — **7%**

Rashid declares you family. You will never spend another Eid alone.

### Success — Neighbourhood Welcome
**أهلاً فيك دايماً** — Points 8–12, Warmth ≥ 55 — **24%**

Rashid tells the neighbours about you. Doors will open before you knock.

### Mixed — Polite Visitor
**تَفَضَّل وقت ما تبي** — Points 3–7 — **47%**

A nice visit. Rashid was generous — he always is — but warmth didn't become bond.

### Failed — Missed Blessing
**الله كريم** — Points ≤ 2 — **22%**

Rashid smiles politely. الله كريم is what Gulf Arabs say when something disappointing happens and they choose grace over complaint.
