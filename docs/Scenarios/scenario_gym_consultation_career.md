# Scenario Design: The Gym Consultation
**ID:** `gym-consultation`
**Mode:** Career
**Level:** Intermediate
**Scenes:** 6 + 1 Secret Bonus Scene
**Endings:** 4 (including 1 secret ending)
**Estimated time:** 15 minutes
**Dialect:** Emirati Gulf Arabic

---

## Premise

Sultan Al Mansouri has a 7:30 AM consultation at your gym. The doctor told him to lose weight. He's carrying ten years of desk work and wants to start over.

Most trainers see the appointment. You see the man.

This scenario teaches professional Arabic in a sales and client-care context — how to greet, reassure, recommend, and close — all in Gulf Arabic. Hidden within it is a career-defining secret: Sultan works at a government entity with 200 employees who need exactly what you offer.

> *"In the Gulf, the client is never just the client. He's the door."*

---

## Character

**Sultan Al Mansouri** — Emirati man, early forties. Government employee. Self-conscious about his weight but self-assured in professional settings. He came because his doctor told him to — not because he wanted to. He is testing whether you are someone worth trusting with a sensitive personal goal.

Sultan uses masculine address throughout. He knows how to read people. A single dismissive or condescending line and the consultation is effectively over.

---

## Scene Breakdown

---

### Scene 01 — The Greeting
**Setting:** Gym front desk — Tuesday 7:30 AM

**Sultan's line:**
> **السلام عليكم. أنا سلطان. عندي موعد استشارة**
> *"Peace be upon you. I'm Sultan. I have a consultation appointment."*

**Teaching moment:** Sultan greeted you in Arabic with the Islamic greeting. Responding correctly — returning وعليكم السلام, using his name immediately, and offering a seat with تفضل — signals that you see a person, not an appointment.

| Choice | Arabic | What it signals | Score |
|--------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| **A — And peace! Welcome Sultan. Please have a seat** ✓ | وعليكم السلام! أهلاً وسهلاً فيك يا سلطان. تفضل اقعد | Full greeting + name + تفضل = professional warmth | **Excellent** |
| B — And peace! Hello. How can I help you? | وعليكم السلام! أهلاً. كيف أقدر أساعدك؟ | Good greeting but jumped to business — he already told you why | Good |
| C — Hey! You must be Sultan. Welcome! (English) | هاي! ولكم! | He opened in Arabic — English reply ignores his choice | Neutral |
| D — Yeah, sit over there. I'll come to you | إي، اقعد هناك. بايي لك | No greeting, no تفضل — he feels like a number | Bad |

---

### Scene 02 — Understanding the Goal
**Setting:** Consultation area — after water offered

**Sultan's line:**
> **الدكتور قالي لازم أنحف. أبي أنزل عشر كيلو عالأقل**
> *"The doctor told me I need to lose weight. I want to drop at least ten kilos."*

**Teaching moment:** Sultan came because his doctor sent him. Acknowledging his goal with إن شاء الله and following up with a professional question validates him without overwhelming him. Commenting on his appearance before asking any questions is the worst possible move.

| Choice | Arabic | What it signals | Score |
|--------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| **A — God willing we can help. 10 kilos is doable. What's your weight now?** ✓ | إن شاء الله نقدر نساعدك. عشر كيلو شي ممكن. كم وزنك الحين؟ | Validate + إن شاء الله + professional follow-up | **Excellent** |
| B — MashaAllah that you came! That's the most important thing | ما شاء الله إنك جيت! | Praising his decision honours it — then collaborative question | Good |
| C — Okay. What's your weight? What's your height? | أوكي. كم وزنك؟ كم طولك؟ | Efficient but cold — intake form, not conversation | Neutral |
| D — Yeah, it's obvious you need exercise. Don't worry | إي واضح إنك تحتاج تمارين | Never comment on a client's body unsolicited | Bad |

---

### Scene 03 — His Background
**Setting:** Consultation area — discussing history

**Sultan's line:**
> **صراحة ما أتمرن من أيام الجامعة. ودي أرجع زي أول**
> *"Honestly, I haven't exercised since university. I'd like to get back to how I was."*

**Teaching moment:** This is a vulnerable admission. Normalizing his situation without condescension, then asking about injuries, shows you care about safety first. Prescribing daily cardio to someone who hasn't moved in a decade is overwhelming and disrespectful.

> **FLAG_1 is set here** if the learner chooses Choice B — mentioning "many clients in your same situation" positions you as someone running a real practice. This is one of three flags needed to unlock the secret ending.

| Choice | Arabic | What it signals | Score |
|--------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| A — A lot of people start from zero. Any injuries? | وايد ناس يبدون من الصفر. ما عندك إصابات؟ | Normalized + safety question = professional care | Excellent |
| **B — I have many clients in your situation. Their results are great. Any injuries?** ✓ | أنا عندي زبايين كثير نفس وضعك | Social proof + injury question → **FLAG_1 set** | Good |
| C — The good thing is you decided to start. That's half the journey | الزين إنك قررت تبدا | Motivational but no health follow-up | Good |
| D — You need cardio every day and a strict diet | تحتاج كارديو كل يوم وداييت صارم | Overwhelming and presumptuous for a beginner | Bad |

---

### Scene 04 — The Program
**Setting:** Consultation area — planning

**Sultan's line:**
> **ثلاث مرات بالأسبوع تمام. شو تقترح؟**
> *"Three times a week works. What do you suggest?"*

**Teaching moment:** Sultan told you what he can commit to — three times per week. Ignoring that and pushing for five shows you weren't listening. Giving a clear, structured recommendation with "we start light" for safety is the mark of a trainer who respects both fitness science and his client's confidence.

| Choice | Arabic | What it signals | Score |
|--------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| **A — أقترح لك: one day cardio, two days weights. We start light** ✓ | أقترح لك ثلاث مرات. يوم كارديو، يومين حديد. ونبدا خفيف | Clear structure + "we start light" for safety | **Excellent** |
| B — Let's build around your level. What do you prefer? | خلنا نسوي برنامج على حسب مستواك | Giving choices respects him; beginners also need direction | Good |
| C — Honestly at your level, I'd say five times per week | صراحة بمستواك الحين، أقترح خمس مرات | Honest but overrides his stated commitment — not listening | Good |
| D — We'll do super sets, drop sets, and HIIT to start | نسوي سوبر ستس، دروب ستس، وكارديو HIIT | Jargon overwhelms someone returning after years away | Bad |

---

### Scene 05 — Pricing
**Setting:** Consultation area — Sultan asks about cost

**Sultan's line:**
> **حلو. عجبني الكلام. بكم الجلسة؟**
> *"Nice. I like what I'm hearing. How much per session?"*

**Teaching moment:** Transparent pricing — giving both the single-session price and the package deal clearly — lets Sultan make an informed comparison. This is critical because Sultan may be building a corporate proposal in his head. Hiding the per-session price or inflating the opening number to "leave room for negotiation" destroys trust in Gulf culture.

> **FLAG_2 is set here** if the learner chooses Choice A — giving both single-session and package price transparently.

| Choice | Arabic | What it signals | Score |
|--------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| **A — 300/session. Better deal: 12 sessions for 3,000 instead of 3,600** ✓ | الجلسة الوحدة بـ ٣٠٠. باقة ١٢ جلسة بـ ٣٠٠٠ بدال ٣٦٠٠ | Both prices + clear savings → **FLAG_2 set** | **Excellent** |
| B — Best deal is 12 sessions for 3,000 dirhams | أحسن شي باقة ١٢ جلسة بـ ٣٠٠٠ | Package only — missing single-session for corporate comparison | Good |
| C — Before the price, let's define your goals | قبل السعر خلنا نحدد أهدافك | Sultan asked directly — deflecting feels evasive | Neutral |
| D — Sessions are 500 dirhams. But we can work something out | الجلسة بـ ٥٠٠ بس ممكن نتفاهم | Starting high to negotiate is dishonest — Gulf Arabs start from honest numbers | Bad |

---

### Scene 06 — Final Close
**Setting:** Consultation area — Sultan wants assurance

**Sultan's line:**
> **حلو. بس شو أحسن سعر تقدر تسويه لي؟ ودي أتأكد قبل ما أدفع**
> *"Nice. But what's the best price you can do? I want to be sure before I pay."*

**Teaching moment:** Sultan is not negotiating price — he is testing confidence. Dropping the price instantly signals desperation. Offering a free trial instead shows you believe in the product and gives Sultan everything he needs to pitch a corporate package to his HR team without any financial risk.

> **FLAG_3 is set here** if the learner chooses Choice A — offering a free trial instead of a discount.

| Choice | Arabic | What it signals | Score |
|--------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| **A — First two days are free. Get to know me and my style. Then decide** ✓ | أول يومين مجاناً. تتعرف عليّ وعلى أسلوبي. بعدها تقرر | Value over discount — confidence → **FLAG_3 set** | **Excellent** |
| B — I can do 12 sessions for 2,800. That's my best price | أقدر أسوي لك ١٢ جلسة بـ ٢٨٠٠ | Fair discount but asking him to commit without trying | Good |
| C — The price is fixed, but the quality is worth it | السعر ثابت، بس الجودة تستاهل | Firmness is fine; claiming quality without proof is empty | Neutral |
| D — Okay fine, I'll do 200 per session | أوكي خلاص أسوي لك ٢٠٠ | Instant drop = desperation — he'll undervalue the service | Bad |

---

### Scene 07 (BONUS / SECRET) — The Corporate Offer
**Setting:** Gym exit — Sultan pauses at the door
**Access:** Only available when FLAGS 1, 2, and 3 are all set AND total score ≥ 38

**Sultan's line:**
> **اسمع... أنا أشتغل في جهة حكومية. عندنا فوق الـ ٢٠٠ موظف. صراحة وايد منهم يحتاجون تمارين. لو أسوي لك عقد مع الشركة، تقدر تسوي جلسات للموظفين؟**
> *"Listen... I work at a government entity. We have over 200 employees. Honestly, a lot of them need exercise. If I set up a contract, could you do sessions for the staff?"*

**Teaching moment:** Sultan did not plan to say this. You earned it — by positioning yourself as someone running a real practice (FLAG_1), giving him transparent pricing he could cite in a proposal (FLAG_2), and offering a free trial that removed his personal financial risk (FLAG_3). He trusts you enough to bring you to his organisation.

| Choice | Arabic | Outcome |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **A — Of course! Let me prepare a corporate quote for you** ✓ | طبعاً أقدر! خلني أجهز لك عرض سعر للشركة | Corporate contract unlocked |
| **B — Absolutely! Let's connect on WhatsApp** ✓ | أبشر! نتواصل على الواتس | Corporate contract unlocked |

Both choices unlock the secret ending.

## Endings

### Secret Exceptional — The Corporate Contract
**Arabic:** العقد المؤسسي *(The Corporate Contract)*
**Minimum score:** 38
**Required flags:** FLAG_1 + FLAG_2 + FLAG_3
**Secret:** true — *"Only 8% of users discover this."*

Sultan didn't just sign up for personal training — he opened the door to a corporate wellness contract with his government entity. You didn't chase the sale. You built the case.

**Cultural journey shown:**
- Positioning: Mentioning "many clients" established you as a real practitioner
- Transparency: Both per-session and package pricing gave Sultan data for corporate proposals
- Value, not price: A free trial proved your confidence — no desperate discounting
- Result: A one-person consultation became a 200-person opportunity

---

### Success — The Signed Client
**Arabic:** الزبون الموقع *(The signed client)*
**Minimum score:** 24

Sultan signed up. Your consultation was professional and culturally aware. He's committed to the package and will show up tomorrow morning. But there was a bigger opportunity in this conversation that you didn't unlock.

---

### Mixed — The Maybe
**Arabic:** يمكن *(Maybe)*
**Minimum score:** 12

Sultan was polite but unconvinced. "Let me think about it" in Gulf culture usually means he's comparing you to another trainer. The consultation was adequate but didn't build enough trust to close.

---

### Failed — The Lost Lead
**Arabic:** الفرصة الضايعة *(The lost opportunity)*
**Minimum score:** 0

Sultan is gone. The إن شاء الله without a date tells you everything — he's not coming back. He needed warmth before business, transparency before commitment, respect before expertise.

## Cultural Notes for Reviewers

1. **Returning the Islamic greeting** — Sultan opened with السلام عليكم. This is not just a greeting — it is a statement of shared identity. Responding in English ignores it.

2. **Never comment on a client's body unsolicited** — Sultan is already self-conscious. Even a light comment ("obviously you need exercise") before any questions crosses a professional and personal line.

3. **Transparent pricing in Gulf culture** — Gulf Arabs do not negotiate from inflated numbers. Starting at 500 when your price is 300 signals you are not trustworthy. The honest number is always the opening number.

4. **Free trial over discount** — Dropping price signals desperation. Offering value (a free trial) signals confidence. Sultan needs to propose this to his HR department — he needs a story, not a deal.

5. **FLAG system** — The three flags are subtle signals that Sultan interprets subconsciously. None of them feel like sales tactics at the time — they are simply the right professional instincts applied in the right order.
