## PREMISE

Ahmed, your Emirati colleague, stops by your desk: coffee. Not a small question. In Gulf work culture, a coffee invitation is the entry point to every meaningful professional relationship.

---

## CHARACTERS

**Ahmed** — Emirati, early forties. Well-connected. Generous, observant, decisive. Invites once. Doesn't follow up if the first is mishandled.

**Omar** — Sudanese, mid-thirties. Ahmed's longtime colleague. Joins in Scene 3. Speaks with Sudanese markers: ياخي (yākhī, "brother"), شنو (shenu, "what"), زول (zōl, "person/guy"), كيفك يا زول.

## SCENE 03 — Omar Joins ⚠️ DIVERGENCE SCENE

**Setting:** Coffee corner. Omar walks in, greets Ahmed warmly.

**Omar (Sudanese):** يا زول! كيفك يا أحمد؟ شنو الأخبار؟ *(yā zōl! kīfak yā Aḥmad? shenu l-akhbār?)*
**Ahmed (to you):** هذا عمر، أخوي مِن أيام الجامعة

**Teaching:** Omar's Sudanese is different from Gulf — يا زول, شنو, كيفك. Engaging him in Arabic (even one phrase) shows you're not just here for Ahmed.

| Choice | Arabic | Romanization | T | R | C |
|--------|--------|--------------|---|---|---|
| A | أهلاً يا زول! تَشَرَّفنا — شنو أخبارَك انت؟ | ahlan yā zōl! tasharrafnā — shenu akhbārak inta? | +3 | +3 | +3 |
| B | أهلاً عمر، تَشَرَّفنا | ahlan ʿOmar, tasharrafnā | +2 | +2 | +2 |
| C ⚠️ | Hi Omar, nice to meet you | hi ʿOmar, nice to meet you | **0** | **+1** | **-2** |
| D | *(nod, keep talking to Ahmed)* | — | -2 | -2 | -2 |

**⚠️ C divergence:** polite by English standards (Respect +1) but switching to English when greeted in Arabic by a Sudanese colleague signals you only do Arabic for Emiratis.

## SCENE 04 — The Family Question

**Setting:** Omar excuses himself. Ahmed leans back.

**Ahmed:** عندك أهل هني ولا في البلاد؟

**Teaching:** Family questions in Gulf workplaces are not personal — they're foundational. Refusing reads as antisocial. Asking back about *his* family shows you understand the exchange.

| Choice | Arabic | Romanization | T | R | C |
|--------|--------|--------------|---|---|---|
| A | الحمد لله — في البلاد، بس دايماً في قلبي. وانت يا أحمد؟ عيالَك بخير؟ | al-ḥamdu li-llāh — fī l-blād, bass dāyman fī qalbī. w-inta yā Aḥmad? ʿyālak bi-khayr? | +3 | +3 | +3 |
| B | في البلاد. الله يَحفَظهم | fī l-blād. Allāh yiḥfaẓhum | +2 | +2 | +2 |
| C | في البلاد — وايد أشتاق لهم | fī l-blād — wāyid ashtāq lahum | +1 | +2 | +2 |
| D | أُفَضِّل ما نَتكلّم عن أمور شخصية | ufaḍḍil mā natkallam ʿan umūr shakhṣiyya | -2 | -2 | -2 |

## ENDINGS

### Exceptional — Family Partnership (أنت من أهلنا)
**Total ≥ 28, all meters ≥ 8** — **6%**

Ahmed invites you to meet his family this weekend. Crossing from "colleague" to "family" happens rarely.

### Success — Job Referral (إن شاء الله خير)
**Total 16–27, all meters ≥ 4** — **24%**

Ahmed mentions an opening and says he'll recommend you personally. A Gulf personal recommendation outweighs any CV.

### Mixed — Transactional Colleague (زين، شوف)
**Total 4–15** — **48%**

Pleasant chat, professional ceiling. Nothing made him want to go further.

### Failed — Missed Connection (بكرة إن شاء الله)
**Total ≤ 3** — **22%**

Ahmed politely closes the conversation. He won't invite again.
